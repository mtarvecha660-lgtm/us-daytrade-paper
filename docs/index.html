How it works

  • During US market hours (9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET = 8:00 PM–2:30 AM IST) GitHub runs the bot about every 5 minutes. It reads free Yahoo 5-minute bars. No broker, no keys, no real orders.
  • Scanner: each stock’s opening range is its 9:30–9:45 AM high and low. A hit is the first 5-minute bar that closes above the range high (from 9:50 AM, no new entries after 3:30 PM).
  • Entry: on a fresh hit the bot buys at the next bar’s open, most-traded stock first, one trade at a time, max 3 a day.
  • Exit plan: stop at the middle of the opening range, target 1R (as far above the entry as the stop is below), and always sold by 3:55 PM ET.
  • Size & risk: the skill’s own limits for a $10,000 account: max $100 loss a trade (1%), $300 a day (3%), 75% max in one stock, 10% always in cash, whole shares.
  • Costs: no commission (like zero-commission US brokers), but every market order pays slippage of 2 bps (min 1 cent) a share.
  • Data delay: the bot only uses bars that closed at least a minute ago, so it acts 1–6 minutes behind the market, plus however late GitHub starts the run.
  • R = the trade’s result divided by its planned risk (+1R = target hit, −1R = stopped out). The rules are frozen in EVALUATION.md; backtest in RESULTS.md.

Paper results don’t predict real results; most day traders lose money. Not financial advice. Code & raw data on GitHub
